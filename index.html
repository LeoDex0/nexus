Convergence
    Vision Essay, 2035

    Infrastructure that thinks will outlast infrastructure that is merely strong.

    For two centuries we built for the worst case. Thicker cables, wider roads, larger reserves. Strength was the only defence against uncertainty, and strength is expensive: most of what we have built sits idle, waiting for a day that rarely comes.

    Computation changes that trade. A grid that sees a storm forming can move power out of its path. A port that knows tomorrow's arrivals can berth them without a queue. Capacity becomes a question of timing rather than size.

    By 2040 we expect the most valuable infrastructure on Earth to be invisible: a layer of reasoning underneath the physical world, keeping it within limits we agree on together. We are building that layer slowly and in public, with a human signature on every decision that cannot be undone.
